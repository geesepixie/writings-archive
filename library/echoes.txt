I believe that the evil embodied in narcissists cannot be changed; it must bear responsibility for its fate—for the sake of the natural order. Otherwise, disorder and corruption follow.

When society grants evil responsibility, it only grows in influence, leading to public decay: a culture that worships false idols born from the immorality of the collective unconscious. Those who cannot cultivate God’s presence within themselves end up praising demons out of weakness.

Narcissists have no ideals. They are driven by destructive instincts, and their hunger for power links directly to authoritarian structures. People who cannot handle freedom become trapped in fear, enslaved and exploited by these same narcissists.

Authoritarianism is not growth—it is self-destruction. Repression produces only conflict, for growth is impossible without sacrifice. True consciousness begins when a person understands their role in society and seeks to make it better.

This is the conflict: authoritarianism versus mindfulness. And sacrifice is the law that cannot be avoided. The question is: must we sacrifice ourselves to save narcissists—or is our duty to condemn them without mercy?
(I mean public condemnation here, not a call for violence—just to clarify.)

The Bodhisattvas seek to save all beings from suffering, but narcissists are an evil that cannot be saved. Can we remain idle in their presence? This is one of the hardest questions a human can face.

Nietzsche saw society moving toward destruction. In that sense, narcissism may play the role of “necessary evil,” forcing change. But how necessary is it? Have we not already surrendered? To defeat evil, it must be fought.

Suffering, I believe, will always be absorbed and integrated by the universe, which naturally tends toward growth. This path to wholeness—individuation—requires sacrifice. Every individual must take responsibility for their suffering. This is the path to becoming human.

Humanity’s role, then, is to strive for the common good. I think of Dune, where Paul Atreides endures the test of the box that contains only pain. It is a perfect metaphor for mindfulness: “We test for humans to find the person.”

For me, the birth of humanity is the transcendence of its animal nature and the devotion to a higher goal. That goal must always lead society toward growth. And the only rule is this: an idea must not betray itself, or it becomes the very evil it seeks to fight.
