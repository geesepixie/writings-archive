They tuned the hall with borrowed cheer,
A lilting song, a chandelier.
The floor was cold, the candles kind,
They asked me only for my mind.

“Come dance,” they said, “no need to ache,
No vows to keep, no bones to break.
Just lighter steps, no past, no name—
We all look lovely, much the same.”

The music smiled. I almost did.
The room felt safe. The door was hid.
Each turn erased a shade of weight,
Each pause pretended this was fate.

But something in the rhythm stayed—
Too smooth, too carefully obeyed.
No breath between the count and start,
No space for doubt, no room for heart.

The dancers moved like time forgot,
Not warm, not cold — just what-is-not.
Their faces held a practiced calm,
Like something healed without a scar.

The rhythm lingered, thin and sweet,
A place between the heart and feet.
I felt the pull. I felt it fade.
I stayed, or turned —
The song still played.
