As she wanders among the clocks, a ghost lingering in hours that no one claims,
I wonder if she remembers what it was she left behind, or if she only drifts.
Each tick stretches endlessly like a corridor she cannot escape, each chime a door that refuses to open,
and I think—what is time but a house abandoned, its mirrors holding only the faint trace of a presence already fading?

Minutes pass around her, unnoticed, untouched, the world elsewhere moving on with careless swiftness,
and yet here she moves, fragile as a breath that fogs a glass and disappears, poised in careful solitude,
that questions whether solitude itself is punishment or preservation, whether silence is a wound or a sanctuary.

Does she tend to what was left behind, or is it the remnants of the world that tend to her,
the debris of lived hours, the whispers of moments that refused to stay?
And I ask myself—are we all not ghosts among clocks,
haunted by what we cannot reclaim, carrying in our fragile hands the echoes of a time surrendered to silence?
